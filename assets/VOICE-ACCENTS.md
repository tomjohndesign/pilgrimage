# Old English voice direction

The Dutch-influenced takes are the user's preferred artistic treatment. They
are now the peasant selection voices. That preference does not establish that
the recordings reproduce historical pronunciation.

## What Dutch can help with

Daniel Paul O'Donnell's Old English pronunciation guide explains that several
Old English long vowels have values familiar from continental languages,
including Dutch, German, French and Italian, rather than their modern English
descendants. Vowel duration also matters. This gives a plausible reason for a
continental treatment to sound less like modern English, but does not measure
the accuracy of these generated takes. [University of Lethbridge guide](https://people.uleth.ca/~daniel.odonnell/tutorials/the-pronunciation-of-old-english).

Historical kinship is a separate question. Old English, Old Frisian and, to a
lesser degree, Old Saxon shared innovations around the North Sea. A modern Dutch
accent is not interchangeable with those earlier language varieties. Nor does
an Icelandic accent establish Old English authenticity. [Research on North Sea
Germanic features](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/frisians-and-their-north-sea-neighbours/geography-and-dialects-of-old-saxon-riverbasin-communication-networks-and-the-distributional-patterns-of-north-sea-germanic-features-in-old-saxon/5CCE39B84DADACEA0BFE94D2FD3B20B0).

Old English itself covered several dialects and centuries. We should target a
region and period before describing the result as a reconstruction. The
pronunciation guide from the Society for Name Studies in Britain and Ireland
explicitly describes its own guidance as approximate. [SNSBI guide](https://www.snsbi.org.uk/exploring-names/old-english-pronunciation/).

## Current audition choices

- **Dutch:** preferred treatment; three neutral lines for each body voice.
- **Yorkshire:** a modern regional alternative, chosen for atmosphere.
- **West Country:** another modern regional alternative, chosen for atmosphere.
- **IPA baseline and Icelandic:** previous comparisons remain available.

Yorkshire and West Country each have a short and a longer line in both voices.
They retain the same IPA target and actor as the baseline; only accent direction
changes. Stochastic generation means this is an audition, not a controlled
phonetic experiment. The user explicitly accepts modern English accents as an
artistic exception to historical accuracy. No accent is ranked as the surviving
"real Anglo-Saxon" accent.

The next accuracy check should listen for vowel duration, consonants and word
endings against a specialist's reading. An accent label, an IPA prompt and
successful playback alone cannot verify those features.
